# HivePulse features

The full list of what HivePulse does today, where to find it, and which document describes it.
The same list is shown to users at `/release-notes` on the website (source: `lib/releaseNotes.ts`), and
the apps open that page from **Settings → Release notes**. Keep the three in step: when a feature ships,
add it to `lib/releaseNotes.ts` (a line in `RELEASES`, and in `FEATURES` if it is new), write or extend its
help page in `app/[locale]/help/[slug]/content/`, add a News entry in `lib/news.ts`, and update this file.

Platforms: **W** website, **I** iPhone app, **A** Android app.

The content is `backend/app/beekeeping_year.py` (dates for central Germany), the arithmetic `backend/app/season.py`.
The ribbon's arithmetic is `lib/yearRibbon.ts`, ported to `YearRibbon.kt` and `YearRibbon.swift`; the three
test suites mirror each other case for case, because the same year has to come out the same shape on all
three. The photographs and their credits are `lib/yearPhotos.ts`, `YearPhotos.kt` and `YearPhotos.swift`,
all four files generated by `scripts/fetch_year_photos.py` and listed in `docs/photo-credits.md` -- most
are CC BY or CC BY-SA, so a credit that drifted between clients would be a licence breach. The apps carry
the pictures inside the app (1.2 MB), because at the apiary there is usually no signal. Five jobs still
have no usable photograph: issue 412.

The questionnaire is a web page on purpose and the apps only link to it: it needs no account, it is
filled in once, and it is 35 questions long, so a native copy on two platforms would be a large amount
of duplicated screen for something nobody does twice. What an app is good for is asking the question
where somebody is already looking at the dates and thinking "not here" -- so both year screens carry
the invitation, in the reader's language, and it opens the browser.

Nothing a beekeeper sends moves a date: `backend/app/year_feedback.py` groups the ratings and
`routers/admin.py` offers the groups. The direction of a correction is the verdict's, not the number's
-- `too_early` means the calendar is too early, so the entry belongs later -- and the server replaces
whatever sign it was sent (`YearRatingIn.clean`). The clients never ask anybody for a sign either
(`yearShiftDays`): a signed field whose direction another field already fixes is a field that gets
filled in backwards, and one backwards client would pull a median that otherwise agrees back towards
zero.
Guide values only; medicines subject to the approvals of the country.

### The treatment record

`GET /apiaries/{id}/treatment-record?year=YYYY` returns a PDF: every treatment carried out at that
apiary in that year, with the product, the amount, how many colonies, the planned and the actual
date and who recorded it, and the year's varroa readings beside them. Part of the subscription,
judged by the apiary's **owner**, so somebody invited onto an apiary can print the paperwork for
work they did.

Beekeepers in the EU must record the veterinary medicines they use, because bees are
food-producing animals (Art. 108(5) of Regulation (EU) 2019/6; in Germany §§ 1–2
Tierhalter-Arzneimittelanwendungs- und Nachweisverordnung and § 95a(3) TAMG). Five years, shown
on request.

Three things decide whether the document is any good:

* **The amount is free text, not a number with a unit.** A dose is written the way the product is
  applied — "2 × 15 ml per colony", "60 g", "one strip per 5 frames".
* **The number of colonies is frozen when a treatment is marked done.** Counting them when the
  record is printed would count the colonies that exist *then*: ten treated in August and three
  given away in October would read as seven, which is a false statement in a document an authority
  may ask to see.
* **It is a document to keep, not a claim of compliance.** The regulation allows an electronic
  record only where the data cannot be altered afterwards, which a document regenerated from
  editable rows is not — but it allows loose leaves. So HivePulse prints the sheet, dated, and the
  beekeeper keeps it. Nothing in the product says more, and nothing should start to.

A missing amount prints as a dash and the document says in its own line how many entries are
incomplete. A year with nothing treated still produces a PDF: "nothing was treated here in 2026"
is exactly what somebody may need to show.

The PDF is typeset with DM Sans, which lives in `backend/app/assets/fonts/` because ReportLab's
own fonts cannot draw `ą ę ż ś ń ź` — four languages would have looked perfect while Polish
quietly fell apart.

## What the supporter subscription includes

Decided while the application had no users at all, because a quantity limit cannot be introduced
once people have crossed it (#478). €19.99 a year; the technical side is #17.

| Free, permanently | With a subscription |
|---|---|
| up to **10 colonies**, apiaries unlimited | unlimited |
| QR codes, scanning, offline inspections | the treatment record and yearly report (#477) |
| the beekeeping year, moves, reminders | **inviting** somebody to an apiary or a hive |
| the hornet tracker, in full | custom fields |
| statistics for the last 365 days | the whole history, and the regional health map |
| the raw data export | |

Three rules hold the whole thing together, and they are enforced in `backend/app/limits.py`:

* **Reaching a limit never closes what already exists.** Not hidden, not read-only, not deleted —
  the only thing that stops is adding the eleventh colony. Somebody who was a supporter and is not
  any more keeps every colony they had. A paywall that locks away work already done is the one
  thing this kind of product does not survive, and it would be a poor thing to do to a record of
  living animals.
* **The person who invites pays; the guest does not.** A feature that needs two paying people
  sells to nobody. An apiary's custom fields go by its *owner's* subscription for the same reason.
* **Statistics are narrowed, not refused.** `preset=all` from a free account answers with a year.
  A page that says 403 teaches somebody the app is broken; one that shows less teaches them what
  the subscription is for.

**Collaborators are deliberately unlimited, and not priced per head.** A colony count measures how
serious *you* are; a collaborator count measures how many *other people* you bring in, and each of
those is a potential subscriber in their own right — charging per guest taxes exactly the thing that
grows the user base. There is no cost to recover either: a guest has no account of their own, they
sit on somebody else's apiary. Abuse is already closed from two sides: only a paying account can
invite at all, and `shares.py` allows 20 invitations an hour.

The second tier that does exist is **not "more collaborators" but the association or the commercial
operation** — a different customer who wants a shared holding, *one* invoice and an export for the
association's records. That is worth selling as its own product when the first one asks; a seat
counter would overcharge the hobbyist with a partner and undercharge the association. It can wait,
because unlike a quantity limit a *higher* tier takes nothing away from anyone and stays addable
later.

What is deliberately *not* behind it: the hornet tracker (reporting *Vespa velutina* helps
everybody, including people who will never pay), the beekeeping year (the content nobody else
has), QR codes and scanning (the moment the app makes sense), and the raw export of one's own
data (a position, not a feature — and in the EU a right). What is sold is the *document*, not the
data.

### Buying it

`/dashboard/subscription` on the website shows the state, the renewal or expiry date, the colony
counter, what the subscription includes and where to cancel. The purchase link is built **on the
server** (`GET /subscriptions/web-checkout`): the account id is a path segment of it, a link
without one answers 404 rather than selling anonymously, and a link with the *wrong* one takes
money and attaches it to nobody. Buying inside the apps goes through StoreKit and Play Billing, as
those stores require; whichever way it is bought, the entitlement is derived on the server and
works on all three clients.

A subscription sold by Apple or Google can only be cancelled there. The server says so
(`store_managed`), so the three clients do not each decide for themselves what they may offer.

Reaching the limit is answered with an amber notice and a way to the subscription page, never a red
error banner — it is an offer, not a fault. A client tells the two apart by the error **code**,
never by the sentence, which exists in five languages.

## Account

Sign in with e-mail, Apple or Google; English, German, French, Spanish and Polish; change password (not
for accounts made through Apple or Google); delete the account and all data (also revokes Apple access);
crash reports to an EU-region Sentry; a guided tour.

**Send feedback** sits in Settings under Help on all three clients. On Android it opens the Play listing,
because feedback sent from there is what reaches the Play Console's testing feedback page; on the iPhone
and the website it opens a mail with the version and build already in the subject.

## Navigation (the same on iPhone and Android)

Tabs: Apiaries, Scan, Hornets, Members, Settings. Apiary list toolbar: statistics, QR batches, map of
moves, beekeeping year. Every create action is the amber labelled button at the bottom right.
Settings: custom fields, profile, change password, reminders, region, export, admin, help and tour, release
notes, diagnostics, log out, danger zone. See `CLAUDE.md`.
